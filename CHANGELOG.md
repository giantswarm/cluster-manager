## [Unreleased]

### Added

- OTLP traces (giantswarm/giantswarm#36711). The server exports its spans when `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` is set, through `github.com/giantswarm/mcp-toolkit/tracing`: a server span per HTTP request (`POST /mcp`; the probes are not traced), an `mcp.<method>` server span per JSON-RPC request carrying `mcp.method`, `mcp.tool.name` and `gen_ai.tool.name` on `mcp.tools/call`, and a `tool.<name>` span around each tool handler. An inbound `traceparent` (muster's) joins the caller's trace. The chart takes `observability.otel.{endpoint,protocol,headers,resourceAttributes,sampler,samplerArg}` and renders the `OTEL_*` env only with an endpoint: gRPC, `parentbased_traceidratio` at `0.1`, the pod's `k8s.pod.name`, `k8s.namespace.name` and `k8s.node.name` as resource attributes.
- `list_clusters` prices a model cache claim whose StorageClass is gone from the tier annotations on the claim (giantswarm/cluster-manager#99). The connectivity chart's cache StorageClass is Helm-owned and named by a digest of its parameters, so an upgrade changing them, or an uninstall, removes it while the claim and its billed volume stay; the class being the only place the tier was read from, the answer was "the volume's tier cannot be told" and no price. The chart stamps the tier the claim was provisioned with onto it at create (giantswarm/agent-platform#605): `agent-platform.giantswarm.io/volume-type`, `agent-platform.giantswarm.io/volume-iops` and `agent-platform.giantswarm.io/volume-throughput` (MiB/s), the EBS CSI StorageClass parameters `type`, `iops` and `throughput`. A claim's tier is read from its class where the class can be read, whatever the annotations say, else from the annotations; every claim an answer names says which (`tierSource: storageClass | claimAnnotations`), and a claim with neither keeps its `tierNote`.
- `cache` defaults to off (giantswarm/cluster-manager#86): `create_node_pool` and `enable_model_serving` without the argument keep the slice's setting where the cluster's slice release runs, and compose a first slice with `modelServing.cache.enabled: false` — a model cache claim is a volume billed every month it exists, after every pool is removed too, and is never created unasked; `cache: true` is the opt-in, the slice's upgrade where it ran without one. Until now a call that did not mention the cache created the claim.
- The model cache is the cluster's setting with a standing cost, said on every answer, and `remove_model_cache` removes it (giantswarm/cluster-manager#83). A model cache claim is a gp3 volume that outlives every pool and the slice release by design and is billed every month it exists, filled or not — on an installation a 100 GiB claim stood for a day after its last pool went, at about $27 a month at list prices, and no answer named that; nothing removed it, and every refusal naming "remove the claim" as a way out pointed at nothing a caller could do. Now every claim an answer names — `list_clusters`' `serving.readiness.cacheClaims[]`, the writes' `cacheClaim` and `cacheClaims[]` — carries its `capacity` (and `capacityGiB`), `storageClass` and `tier{type, iops, throughputMiBps}` read from the class, `reclaimPolicy`, `created`, and its monthly list price in the cluster's region (`price{monthlyUSD, source, asOf}`, else `priceNote` — a class that is gone leaves the tier unknown and says so; a storage-only figure is never passed off as the price); `list_clusters` marks the claim the slice release mounts (`mounted`). The `cache` block of `create_node_pool` and `enable_model_serving` carries the same for the claim the slice mounts — `exists` and `since` for one as read, else `capacity`, `tier` and `monthlyPriceUSD` from the connectivity chart's defaults at the slice's version, read from the registry — and its note says the cost is billed while the claim exists, after every pool is removed too, until the cache is removed. The slice release being the cluster's one, `cache: false` while the cluster's slice runs with the cache on is refused, `refused{cacheOn{claim, claimName, remedies}}` beside the text: it would switch the cache off for the models served on every pool while the claim stays and keeps costing — leave `cache` on, or remove the cache; a first slice with `cache: false` stands, and `cache: true` on a slice that ran without it is the slice's upgrade for every pool, the note saying so. `remove_model_cache {cluster, claim?, mode, dryRun}` removes the cache in order: the slice release, where it runs with the cache on, upgraded to `modelServing.cache.enabled: false` first (the connectivity chart applies the claim from a hook on every upgrade, so a claim deleted under a slice with the cache on came back), then every `hf-cache*` claim of the serving namespace — or the one named — deleted on the cluster as the caller, the volume going with the claim under the class's `Delete` reclaim policy (a `Retain` volume is a warning); refused while a pod mounts a claim to be removed (`refused{models, hint}`: the served models to unload first — the claim's protection finalizer holds a mounted claim), plainly while the cluster cannot be read as the caller, and where someone else provides serving; the answer names the claims removed with their price (`removedClaims[]`) and what follows (every model downloads and compiles again at its next start, about 90 s more per cold start). The chart's ClusterRole deletes PersistentVolumeClaims and reads StorageClasses. The price table (`hack/aws-on-demand-prices.py`) carries AWS's published EBS gp3 list prices beside the EC2 ones: storage per GiB-month, provisioned throughput per MiB/s-month above the tier's 125 MiB/s, IOPS per IOPS-month above its 3000, for the same regions.
- Any combination of zones stands with the cache on, and the pool follows its cache (giantswarm/cluster-manager#79). `create_node_pool` with several `zones` and the cache on — refused until now, a claim being one volume in one zone — pins the pool's nodes to the zones named and composes the slice mounting the base claim, `hf-cache`: the first predictor binds it to a volume in its node's zone, one of the named, and from then on every predictor mounting it runs there (the scheduler and Karpenter place a pod's node where its volume is); a re-run of the same call pins the pool's nodes to that zone, and `zonesNote` and `cache.note` say so. A claim Bound in exactly one of the named zones pins the pool to it at once, `zonesNote` naming the zones named, the one that stands and the ways out (`cache: false` to run across the zones, one zone alone to serve from it with its own claim). Claims Bound in several of the named zones are refused (`refused{cacheClaims{claims, remedies}}`: the slice mounts one claim, none is chosen for the person), as is the base claim Bound outside every named zone (`refused{cacheZone{claim, claimZone, zones, remedies}}`: name its zone among the zones, one zone, or `cache: false`). No claim is created per named zone up front: a claim binds only when a pod mounts it, and unbound claims are noise. `list_clusters` names each cluster's node-subnet zones (`zones[]`, `zonesNote` when they cannot be read), read from its `AWSCluster` as `create_node_pool` does, so a portal can offer them as choices. On an installation the accelerator family's capacity moved between zones by the hour, and a pool pinned to one zone launched nothing in four of six attempts; a person wanting the capacity of several zones and a kept cache had no call.
- The model cache is one claim per zone, and a pool created with `zones` mounts its zone's claim (giantswarm/cluster-manager#71). `create_node_pool` with one zone and the cache on composes the slice release with `modelServing.cache.pvc.name` naming the claim of that zone — the `hf-cache*` claim Bound there (the one claim of before, in the zone its first predictor was in, reused rather than doubled), else `hf-cache-<zone>` (`<base>-<zone>`, the base being `--serving-cache-claim`), which the connectivity chart creates where it does not exist and keeps: the first predictor of the pool binds it to a volume in the zone, a later pool in the zone reuses it, pools in other zones use theirs. Until now the serving namespace's one claim pinned every pool to its zone, and when that zone had no capacity for the accelerator family — on an installation, three attempts in a row launched nothing there while other zones offered the family the same hour — the ways out were to remove the claim (the cached weights and compiled graphs of every model served so far) or to serve without it (a cold download and compile at every start, 90–180 s of each). Several zones with the cache on are refused, a claim being one volume in one zone (`refused{cacheZone{zones, remedies}}`; with `cache: false` several zones stand); a claim named after the zone but Bound elsewhere is refused as before (`refused{cacheZone{claim, claimZone, zones, remedies}}`). Without `zones` the claims decide as before: one `hf-cache*` claim — Bound, the pool is pinned to its zone and the slice mounts it; not Bound or naming no zone, nothing is pinned and `zonesNote` says so; not readable, a warning —; no claim, no pin; several claims are refused naming each with its zone and phase (`refused{cacheClaims{claims, remedies}}`), asking for `zones`. The answer names the claim the slice mounts (`cache{enabled, claim, note}`; `cacheClaim` as read, `null` while the zone's claim does not exist yet) and every claim of the namespace (`cacheClaims[]`). `list_clusters` names every claim with its zone and phase under `serving.readiness.cacheClaims[]` and the slice release's own setting under `serving.readiness.cache{enabled, claim}`. `list_node_pools`' launch refusal names the claim living in the pinned zone and the re-run that moves the pool: `zones` naming one zone with capacity, whose claim the slice then mounts. `enable_model_serving` keeps the claim its release mounts on a re-run and names it. The slice release is the cluster's one: its predictors mount the claim the last `create_node_pool` with the cache on named. The chart's ClusterRole lists PersistentVolumeClaims for it (it did already).
- The `sizes[]` rows of `create_node_pool` and `list_node_pools` name the node's local NVMe instance store (giantswarm/cluster-manager#69): `instanceStoreGB` as AWS lists it, across `instanceStoreDisks` devices of `instanceStoreDiskGB` each — 250 GB on an `xlarge`, 450 on a `2xlarge`, 600 on a `4xlarge` of g6, g6e and g5; the `8xlarge` of g6 and g6e carry two devices of 450 GB, g5's one of 900; g4dn 125, 225, 225 and 900 — read from `aws ec2 describe-instance-types` into the composer's shape table for every size of the curated families. From gpu-node-pool 0.7.0 a pool node's `/var/lib` is the first device, so `instanceStoreDiskGB` is what a node of the size gives its pods as ephemeral storage (450 GB on a `g6.8xlarge`, not 900). The kserve backend document cluster-manager registers with model-manager (`spec.kserve.gpuPool.instances`) keeps the eight keys model-manager declares: it parses the document strictly, so the answer's price and instance store are never written into it.
- `create_node_pool` takes `zones` and `cache`, and `enable_model_serving` the same `cache` (giantswarm/cluster-manager#65). Where the pool runs is the person's: `zones: ["eu-central-1a"]` pins the pool's nodes to the named availability zones (`pool.zones` on the pool release), checked against the cluster's node subnets — the `AWSCluster`'s `spec.network.subnets` tagged `giantswarm.io/role: nodes`, the ones the chart's EC2NodeClass selects; a zone without one is refused naming the zones the cluster has — and against the model cache claim: a claim Bound in a zone outside the named ones is refused, naming the claim's zone and the ways out (name it among the zones, `cache: false`, remove the claim), beside the text a structured `refused{cacheZone{claim, claimZone, zones, remedies}}` block; a claim inside them pins nothing further. Without `zones` the claim's zone pins the pool as before. `cache: false` (default `true`) serves the slice without the model cache — `modelServing.cache.enabled: false` on the `<cluster>-agent-platform` release: no claim applied or mounted, the weights downloaded into each predictor pod's ephemeral storage, no zone following from a claim, an existing claim left as it is (Helm keeps it) — and the answer says so (`cache{enabled, claim, note}`); a re-run with a changed value is the slice's upgrade; refused where someone else provides serving. On an installation AWS refused the L40S family in the one zone the claim pinned the pool to in three of five runs while other zones offered it the same hour, and Karpenter retries a refused size only after its three-minute unavailable-offering cache — the ways out were to remove the claim or to change accelerator, never to launch elsewhere or to serve without the cache. `zonesNote` says whose the pin is. `list_node_pools`' `nodes` step names every size Karpenter refused — the pool's sizes when the reason is `InsufficientCapacityError` (Karpenter gives a claim up only once every size in every zone of the pool was refused, and cuts its event after the first) — relays the zones AWS named as having the capacity when the message still carries them, and, for a pool pinned by `pool.zones`, what pinned it: the model cache claim's zone (re-run `create_node_pool` with `zones` naming a zone with capacity and `cache: false`, or remove the claim) or the zones named on create (re-run with other zones). `cluster-manager` reads the `AWSCluster` once per call for the region and the node subnets' zones.
- The pool follows the model cache (giantswarm/cluster-manager#59): `create_node_pool` reads the serving namespace's model cache claim (`hf-cache`, the chart's `serving.cacheClaim` / `--serving-cache-claim`) on the cluster as the caller and, with the claim Bound, pins the pool's nodes to the zone its volume's node affinity names — `pool.zones: [<zone>]` on the pool release, the gpu-node-pool chart's `topology.kubernetes.io/zone In` requirement on the Karpenter NodePool, so the prewarm placeholder and every predictor come up there. The claim outlives the pool and is one EBS volume, bound in one zone by the first predictor that mounted it: on an installation a `prewarm: true` pool's placeholder node launched in another zone than the kept claim, the predictor sat Pending on a `ready` pool with an idle node (`1 node(s) didn't match PersistentVolume's node affinity`) and the pool's single-GPU limit forbade a second node. The answer names the pin (`zones`, `zonesNote`: `nodes pinned to eu-central-1b: the model cache (claim model-serving/hf-cache, volume pvc-…) lives there …; remove the cache claim to lift the pin …`) and the claim as read (`cacheClaim{namespace, name, phase, volume, zone}`); `dryRun` shows it and a re-run on an existing pool moves the pin. No claim: no `zones` block. A claim that is not Bound, or whose volume names no zone, pins nothing and `zonesNote` says what was found; a claim or volume not readable as the caller pins nothing and is a warning naming why. `list_clusters` names the claim and its zone under `serving.readiness.cacheClaim`. The chart's ClusterRole (the non-downstream mode) reads PersistentVolumeClaims and PersistentVolumes for it.

### Removed

- `create_node_pool` takes no `teleport` argument, and the composed pool values carry no `teleport` block (giantswarm/cluster-manager#86). The nodes of a pool join Teleport with the cluster's `<cluster>-teleport-join-token` Secret and no other way, as the cluster's own workers do, so the argument overriding it and the read of the Secret's presence were a second path nothing needed; the gpu-node-pool chart the pin moves to joins the nodes unconditionally (giantswarm/gpu-node-pool#24). The read ClusterRole reads Secrets for the kubeconfig's cluster section only.

### Changed

A re-run of `create_node_pool` on an existing pool moves its pin (`would-update`).
- The pinned gpu-node-pool chart moves from 0.6.0 to 0.7.0 (`compose.DefaultPoolChartVersion`), the release whose pool nodes keep `/var/lib` — containerd's image unpacks, the kubelet's directories, the pods' emptyDirs (a model's weights where no cache claim is mounted) and the containers' writable layers — on the node's NVMe instance store instead of a provisioned gp3 volume of 200 GiB at 500 MiB/s and 4000 IOPS created and deleted with the node (giantswarm/cluster-manager#69, giantswarm/gpu-node-pool#21): the chart's default `pool.volumes.libSource: instance-store` — cluster-manager writes no `volumes` block — formats the first `Amazon EC2 NVMe Instance Storage` device as the `lib` filesystem mounted at `/var/lib` at boot, renders no `/dev/xvdd` mapping and sets `instanceStorePolicy: RAID0` on the EC2NodeClass, so Karpenter counts the store as the node's ephemeral storage once the controller building the EC2NodeClass carries the field. Every size of the curated families has one, so no size is refused; a size without one would stop its boot naming the disks present rather than join with `/var/lib` on the root volume. A re-run of `create_node_pool` on an existing pool moves its source's pin (`would-update`) and rolls the pool's nodes.
- The `<cluster>-gpu-operator` release runs on a pool node only the operands a single-GPU serving pool uses (giantswarm/cluster-manager#63): the validator's driver and toolkit validations, the device plugin and GPU feature discovery. Its `validator.cuda.env` and `validator.plugin.env` carry `WITH_WORKLOAD=false` — the operator chart's default (v26.7.0: the validator's `--with-workload` defaults to true and the cuda-validation container sets nothing) started a `nvidia-cuda-validator-*` workload pod on every fresh node beside the device plugin's start; `migManager.enabled: false` — the pool's accelerators (L4, L40S, A10G, T4) have no MIG and the operator schedules mig-manager on MIG-capable GPUs only; `dcgmExporter.enabled: false` — nothing scrapes the exporter out of the box (not the operator chart, the platform chart or the observability stack; a cluster that wants GPU metrics adds a ServiceMonitor by hand), and it was an image pull and a pod initialising DCGM on the GPU while the device plugin brought `nvidia.com/gpu` up. GPU feature discovery stays: model-manager reads its `nvidia.com/gpu.count`, `.memory` and `.product` labels for its node budget. On an installation, node `Ready` → `nvidia.com/gpu` allocatable measured 67 and 75 s with the chart's defaults. The chart's `gpuOperator.dcgmExporter: true` (`--gpu-operator-dcgm-exporter`, `CLUSTER_MANAGER_GPU_OPERATOR_DCGM_EXPORTER`) turns the exporter back on for an installation whose observability scrapes it (a ServiceMonitor on the `nvidia-dcgm-exporter` Service in `kube-system`).

### Fixed

- `create_node_pool` and `enable_model_serving` judge the models host's ClusterIssuer before composing the slice (giantswarm/cluster-manager#110). Where the issuer solves by DNS-01, cert-manager first discovers the zone of `_acme-challenge.models.<domain>` from SOA lookups, walking up past NXDOMAIN; any other answer — on a workload cluster a wildcard CNAME pointing at itself made the challenge name SERVFAIL — fails every order, the Certificate is never issued, and every client fails the TLS handshake while the Gateway reports Programmed. The dry run and the apply now read the ClusterIssuer on the cluster, pick the solver cert-manager picks for the host (`dnsNames`, then the `dnsZones` with the most labels, then one without a selector), run the same SOA walk through the pod's nameservers, and refuse naming the lookup that failed (`SOA lookup of _acme-challenge.models.<domain>. answers SERVFAIL`), as they do for an issuer the cluster does not run or one without a solver for the host; nothing is applied. The answer's `slice.certificate{issuer, solver, zone}` says how the certificate is issued. The chart's ClusterRole gets ClusterIssuers.
- Serving reads not ready while the models listener has no certificate (giantswarm/cluster-manager#110). `list_clusters`' `serving.readiness.modelsGateway.ready` was the Gateway's `Programmed` alone, and a Gateway reads Programmed while its `https` listener has `ResolvedRefs=False` (`InvalidCertificateRef`) because the listener's cert-manager Certificate is never issued: every client then fails the TLS handshake while serving read ready. `ready` is now true only with the Gateway Programmed, the models listener `Programmed` and `ResolvedRefs`, and the Certificate issuing the listener's Secret `Ready`; `reason` and `message` name what holds it back, the pending ACME challenge's type, state and reason included, and the serving evidence says so. The answer carries the parts beside it: `listener{name, hostname, programmed, resolvedRefs, reason}` and `certificate{name, namespace, ready, reason, message, challenge}` (null where no Certificate issues the Secret or the cluster cannot list them).
- `create_node_pool` and `enable_model_serving` read a cluster App's values the way app-operator merges them (giantswarm/cluster-manager#108): the `spec.extraConfigs` ConfigMaps up to priority 50 (25 when unset), `spec.config.configMap`, the entries up to 100, `spec.userConfig.configMap`, the entries above; entries of one priority in list order; a ConfigMap's one key is its values whatever the key's name (`values`, `values.yaml`), as app-operator reads it. Until now only `spec.userConfig.configMap` was read, and an App in the workload clusters' GitOps layout (`spec.config` and `spec.extraConfigs`, no `spec.userConfig`) was refused, a dry run too, although its values carry the pool's snapshot (registry mirrors, proxy, base domain, management cluster, Cilium IPAM mode). Secrets among the sources are still not read. An App naming no values, only Secrets, a ConfigMap that does not exist or one with several keys is refused, naming every source it lists.
- `delete_node_pool` deletes an idle pool while a model waits for a node of another pool (giantswarm/cluster-manager#88). The guard counted every served model with a Pending predictor, so the delete of an idle L4 pool was refused for an L40S model whose own pool had no capacity, and `force` was the only way out. A Pending predictor now counts only for the pool it can land on: the pool its `nodeSelector` or required node affinity names by `giantswarm.io/machine-pool`, else the pool of the NodeClaim Karpenter last nominated it for. A predictor that names no pool still counts for every pool, and with the cluster's last pool every waiting model counts, since the serving slice goes with it.

- With two or more GPU pools on a cluster the kserve backend document names every pool's instance shapes (giantswarm/cluster-manager#89). Until now the second `create_node_pool` wrote the document without `spec.kserve.gpuPool.instances` — no pool being pinned, no sizes were named — and model-manager's fit check answered `fits: false, "no accelerator node"` for a pool scaling from zero, so no model could be loaded against a fresh second pool until its node stood, and the verdict named no size. Now several pools are written as `spec.kserve.gpuPools`, a map from each pool's release name (the node label `giantswarm.io/machine-pool`, `<cluster>-<name>`) to `{instances: [...]}` with the same eight fields as before, one entry per pool release of cluster-manager's, the keys sorted: the pool the call creates or updates with the sizes it composes, the others as their releases' values declare them (the chart's default sizes where they name none); giantswarm/model-manager#152 judges and pins a model against the pool it picks. One pool is the document of before, byte for byte. `create_node_pool` re-writes it on every call, `enable_model_serving` writes the same form, and `delete_node_pool` of a pool that is not the last re-writes the document cluster-manager registered for the cluster for the pools that remain — the one-pool form when one is left — right after the pool's idle nodes and before its own objects (`backend` in the answer, the document among the `manifests`; a `would-update` on a dry run); it never registers a document the cluster has none of.
- A serving slice beside the platform's release admits the platform's callers (giantswarm/cluster-manager#103): on the installation's own cluster the slice's model pods admitted their workload port, 8000, from the slice's own namespace (and the host and remote nodes) alone, so model-manager and the agentgateway data plane in the platform's namespace and the agents' egress gateway in the Substrate namespace were dropped, and a served model could not be reached from the platform. The slice release now sets `modelServing.networkPolicy.additionalIngressNamespaces` to both, read from the platform's release: its `gitops.targetNamespace`, else the namespace the release is deployed in, and its `components.substrate.targetNamespace`, else the chart's `ate-system`. A workload cluster's slice admits nothing more: its callers come through the slice's own models Gateway.
- Reading a model cache claim is free of a data race: the reads of its class and its volume run at once, and a note naming the claim copied the whole claim while the other read filled in its fields; the claim's name is printed before they start.
- The pinned gpu-node-pool chart moves from 0.7.2 to 0.7.4 (`compose.DefaultPoolChartVersion`): 0.7.4's `helm.sh/chart` label is a valid label value for any chart version (giantswarm/gpu-node-pool#27), 0.7.3 bumps the prewarm Job's alpine image. A composed pool renders the same objects as with 0.7.2 but for the label, so no pool node rolls.
- The `helm.sh/chart` label is valid for any chart version (giantswarm/cluster-manager#92): the label is `<name>-<version>` cut to 63 characters, and the cut of a long version (a branch build's `X.Y.Z-dev.<branch>.<date>.<time>.h<sha7>`, or the `<tag>+<digest>` helm-controller installs) could end in `_` or `--.`, which trimming one `-` and one `.` left in place, so the API server refused every labelled object; the whole run of `-`, `.` and `_` at the ends of the cut is now trimmed. `hack/verify-chart.sh`, which `make helm-verify` named but did not exist, renders the label for such versions, and the new `chart` workflow runs it with `helm lint` on every change.
- A forced teardown removes the platform's served models too, finalizer and all (giantswarm/cluster-manager#90). `delete_node_pool` with `force` on a cluster's last pool, and `disable_model_serving` with `force`, take the llm-d controller away from under the models still served; their `LLMInferenceService`s stayed with the controller's `serving.kserve.io/llmisvc-finalizer` on them and nothing left to clear it, and the moment a person stopped one of them it sat terminating for good — the Serving page reading *Stopping* until someone patched the finalizer off by hand (on an installation, for 14 hours). Now, once no controller runs, the served objects model-manager composed in the serving namespace are removed by cluster-manager beside the well-known configs: deleted, the finalizer taken off, through the CRD's storage version (the conversion webhook went with the controller), their workloads going with them through their owner references; a served object already terminating from an earlier forced teardown goes the same way. `objects` lists them, `warnings` says why; a serving object made by hand or through GitOps is named and left. The refusal texts of the guards and the tool descriptions say what `force` does.
- The serving slice composes the llm-d control plane alone (giantswarm/cluster-manager#78): `create_node_pool` and `enable_model_serving` switch on `kserve-llmisvc-crd`, `kserve-llmisvc-resources`, `kserve-runtime-configs` and `modelServing`, and no longer `kserve-crd` and `kserve-resources` — the classic KServe controller agent-platform 4.44.0 removed (giantswarm/agent-platform#574). That release's render refuses a values file carrying the two keys, so every slice composed until now failed its install on an installation whose platform runs 4.44.x (`Helm install failed … the classic KServe controller was removed … its keys are refused: components.kserve-crd, components.kserve-resources`), and no GPU pool could be created there. The slice's platform floor moves from 4.27.0 to 4.44.0, the first release whose `kserve-llmisvc-resources` renders the control plane's shared objects itself; a platform below it is refused naming the floor and why (a slice without the classic components would leave the llm-d controller without its shared config there). They land in the order pool, slice, the slice's backend registration, operator (the ConfigMap right after the slice's HelmRelease), the objects that do not exist yet before the updates, so a call cut short leaves what the re-run completes first. A write about to start with less than a second of the budget left is not started: the answer goes out in time with `partial: true`, the objects not reached as `pending` and `nextStep` naming the re-run, which writes them first. The budget is the request's own deadline when the caller set one, else `--apply-budget` (`CLUSTER_MANAGER_APPLY_BUDGET`, default eight seconds). `enable_model_serving` lands its objects the same way. Every phase — the read fan-out, each write — is timed in the log at debug (`--verbose`), and one line at info says what a write call landed and in how long. The dry run against gazelle went from 9.0 s (two of three calls cancelled) to well under the deadline; the apply's time is the proof's to measure.
- `delete_node_pool` of the cluster's last pool and `disable_model_serving` tear the serving slice down in order, so no `LLMInferenceServiceConfig` is left terminating (giantswarm/cluster-manager#28; giantswarm/giantswarm#37714). Removing the `<cluster>-agent-platform` release at once uninstalled its `kserve-runtime-configs` and `kserve-llmisvc-resources` children in the same instant: KServe's ten well-known configs were deleted while the llm-d controller that clears their `serving.kserve.io/llmisvcconfig-finalizer` was gone, they sat terminating in the release namespace for as long as the CRD lived, and the next slice installed there adopted and lost them — its first `load_model` failed with `ConfigNotFound` (gazelle, 2026-09-16, twice). Now the `kserve-runtime-configs` child release goes first, then the configs are seen gone from the release namespace on the target — waited for (two minutes at most) while the controller runs to clear them, removed by cluster-manager with the finalizer taken off where nothing else will (the controller gone, `force`, or the wait over; a `warnings` entry says which) — and only then the pool, the operator, the slice release and the backend. Without `force` a cluster that cannot be read as the caller is refused before anything is deleted: whether the configs are gone cannot be told.
- `create_node_pool` and `enable_model_serving` heal a cluster whose release namespace holds configs a serving layer left terminating: with no llm-d controller running they are removed, finalizer and all, before the slice lands (`objects` lists them, `warnings` says why), so the slice's release creates them afresh instead of adopting and losing them; with a controller running they are named only — models still referencing them hold them. `list_clusters` names them in the cluster's `serving.evidence` next to the CRDs-only note.
